Dear Friend,

Let's be honest about what happened to you.

A feed is designed to never end. That's the entire business model. The moment you feel finished, you put the phone down, and a finished user is worth nothing to anyone.

So here is the question nobody at Big Dopamine wants asked:

What is the one thing a feed can never give you?

An ending you earned. And a world big enough that you forget to leave.

Short content trains you to leave. Epic fantasy trains you to stay. It is the only genre whose whole job is to build a place bigger than your feed: maps, dynasties, magic systems, three thousand years of buried history. Too big to skim. Too strange to predict.

That's why the Protocol isn't "read more." It's two specific books: 768 pages set in Nyandor, a world of cats with a royal city, a secret police, a Magical Guild that hoards power, and a forbidden gate into every choice ever made.

And there's one more thing you should know about Nyandor.

In the story, that world has already been destroyed. Early in Book One, the narrator (a pipe-smoking cat called the Creator of All Tales) turns to you and explains where the last of it survives:

“The sole place it remains is within this tale. And while you experience this tale, it lives within you. You are its last refuge, its final Construct.”— The Creator of All Tales, The Furbidden Voyd

Big Dopamine wants your attention so it can sell it. Nyandor needs your attention to exist.

That's the Protocol. Two long, strange, serious books, and a world that only works if you stay.

Part I: Softpaw · Chapter I
The Tail's End?

Every cat stands at a fork in the road. It is the curse of our kind to remember the path not taken and to wonder, forever, if it led to a better place.— H.R. Nimblequick, Paws and Effect

Purrhaps… this is death? The notion struck Sory'n like a splash of freezing water. If this endless black expanse was indeed Aurathel, the After Place, it was nothing like what the wise cats promised. No guiding light, no soothing calm, no spirit guardians. And no sign of Leoran, the Creator of All.

Oh, mercy of Great Leoran! What if this isn't Aurathel… What if I wound up in Cathenna! A logical end for the feline who destroyed the world.

Neither heaven nor hell made sense. Wherever she was, she was. At least the thinking part.

One. Two. Three.

I am Sory'n of Tol, and I am a cat.

An insecure, selfish, stupid cat.

But am I dead?

She vocalized. “Mrow.”

The sound left her mouth, but no echo, no resonance followed. The void swallowed it up.

Again, with more force. “Meowwwrrr!”

Terror followed, and in its wake, desperation. A yearning for deliverance, even if it meant actual death.

She is about to find out how she destroyed the world. It takes 375 pages.

Frequently questioned answers

Before you begin.

Is this actually a dopamine reset?

No. That's not really how dopamine works. It's two very long, very good books. We're just describing them in the voice your feed taught you to trust.

Does it contain adaptogens, nootropics or ashwagandha?

No. No. And absolutely not.

Do I have to like cats?

No. One verified reader: “Liking cats will draw some readers in, but is not necessary to appreciate this book.” Another almost didn't pick it up (“Cat fantasy? Really?”) and compared it to The Golden Compass.

Is this a kids' book?

No. It's epic fantasy with an all-cat cast: political marriages, a secret police, Guild-led purges, forbidden magic. It's written for adults. Older teens who like their fantasy serious will be right at home.

Which dose first?

Standard protocol: Dose One, then Dose Two. But each book follows a different reality (in one she fails, in the other she succeeds), and each can be entered on its own. Together they show both sides of the same Voyd.

Paperback or Kindle?

Paperback is the purist's choice: nothing on it can buzz. Kindle is fine too, as long as you don't go looking for the notification shade.

How many cats?
